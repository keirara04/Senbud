Senbud app · onboarding redesign · 16 Oct 2026

First launch in three taps

Today, Welcome leads to a Setup page with five fields at once (period, budget, rent, due day, currency). This concept keeps Welcome, rebrands it around the one number Senbud gives you, and splits Setup into two one-question steps and a reveal. Currency fills itself in from the phone, and rent can be skipped.

  • One question per screenBudget, then rent. Nothing else is asked before Home.
  • Defaults over fieldsCurrency from the phone, month as the period, common amounts as one-tap chips.
  • Show the payoffWelcome shows a sample of the number; the last screen prints yours.
  • Motion explainsPrinting means Bud is working things out; sliding means you moved on. Nothing loops.
What changes

The flow

Welcome → (Welcome back) → Budget → Rent → You’re set → Home.

  • Welcome, rebranded: the wordmark, Bud holding a printed sample (“Safe to spend RM 59.37”), one headline, one line, three promise pills and one button. About half the words of today’s screen.
  • “I’ve used Senbud before” opens a real bottom sheet. Today it expands a card below the fold, so tapping it looks like nothing happens.
  • Setup becomes two steps. Step 1 is the budget only: the period is a tap-to-switch word in the question (“each month”), the amount comes from chips or a keypad, and currency is pre-filled with a Change link. Step 2 is rent, with a Skip, Yes or No cards, chips and a strip of due dates.
  • The copy says rent is included in the budget, because safe-to-spend subtracts rent from it. Without that line a student types spending money only and gets a wrong number on day one.
  • You’re set shows the sum as a receipt: budget, minus rent, days left, then today’s number in highlighter. With RM 1,400, rent RM 450 on the 28th and 16 days left, that’s RM 59.37 (RM 87.50 with no rent), the same floor-to-the-sen maths as safeToSpend.
  • Hourly rate, bills and categories wait until they’re useful, as cards on Home and rows in Settings.
Five frames · light and dark

Storyboard

Each frame is drawn at 375 pt with the app’s tokens. Use the theme switch at the top to check dark mode.

  • One cobalt or highlighter moment per screen: the sample number on Welcome, the printed total on You’re set. Budget and Rent are calm on purpose.
  • Every tap target is at least 48 pt: amount chips, keypad keys, the due-day strip, Yes/No cards.
  • The keypad is the app’s own, so the system keyboard never covers Next.

For whoever builds it

Build notes

Following the ui-styling skill’s principles (tokens, mobile first, focus order, dark mode everywhere) in React Native terms, and its canvas rule of letting the visual carry the message with few words.

Layout and routes

  • One route, /onboarding, with a step state (budget → rent → ready), so Back works and nothing is saved until You’re set.
  • Welcome stays at /welcome; Get started pushes /onboarding; Go to Home replaces to /.
  • Currency from expo-localization (getLocales()[0].currencyCode), falling back to USD; Change opens a picker sheet.
  • Period default is month (today’s Setup defaults to week). Chips: 800/1,000/1,400/2,000 a month, 150/250/350/500 a week, in the local currency.
  • Settings → Budget and rent becomes two rows that open the same steps as sheets, instead of the five-field page.

Components

  • AmountPad (already specced in 4.10) for the budget keypad; rent uses the system decimal pad because it’s optional.
  • QuickPicks: a row of 48 pt chips, role radio, the selected one in accent.
  • DayStrip: a horizontal list of 48 pt day circles centred on the most likely day, role radiogroup, label “Rent due on the 28th”.
  • ChoiceCard: the big Yes/No cards, role radio, 64 pt tall.
  • ReceiptCard plus Bud for Welcome and You’re set; both already exist or are specced.
  • StepHeader: Back, a two-segment progress bar (role progressbar, “Step 1 of 2”) and Skip when the step is optional.

Accessibility

  • VoiceOver reads the question first, then the amount (“RM 1,400, adjustable”), then the chips.
  • The period word in the title is a button labelled “Period: month. Double-tap to switch to week.”
  • The sample number on Welcome is labelled “For example, safe to spend RM 59.37”, so nobody thinks it’s their own.
  • At 200% text the keypad keys stay 48 pt and the title wraps; the chips row scrolls sideways instead of shrinking.
  • Light and dark both use the tokens: periwinkle primary on night, cobalt on paper, receipts white in both.
Before it’s built

Open questions

  1. Two steps, or none? The alternative is no questions at all: land on Home and ask for budget and rent as two cards there. That’s fastest, but Home would open with no number, which is the whole point of the app. This concept keeps two quick steps.
  2. Default period: month or week? Today’s Setup defaults to week; the spec examples and these concepts use month.
  3. Editing later. OK for Settings → Budget and rent to reuse these two steps as sheets, instead of the five-field page?
  4. Sample number on Welcome. Keep the “For example” receipt, or show Bud on its own?
